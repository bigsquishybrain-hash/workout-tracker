⚠️ Read this first

  • Get your surgeon's OK before starting, and ideally a physical therapist who does post-op or post-ostomy core rehab. They can tweak any of this for your hernia.
  • Breathe out on the effort. Never hold your breath and strain, on these or on any machine.
  • Stop if you see a bulge pop out, or a ridge or "doming" along your scar line, or you get new pain at the hernia. Stop that move and tell your doctor.
  • Go to the ER if a bulge becomes hard, painful, won't push back in, or comes with nausea or vomiting.
  • Ask your surgeon whether to wear a hernia support belt while you train.
  • Start easy. You should feel it working, not straining. Quality over reps.
What "gentle brace" means

Breathe out, then lightly draw your belly button in toward your spine, about 20–30% effort, like zipping up snug jeans. Ribs stay down and you keep breathing. Your belly should flatten, never push out. Every move below uses this brace.

Logging: leave Weight blank for bodyweight moves. Put breaths or reps in Reps. For Walking, put minutes in Reps. For Pallof Press, put the cable weight in Weight and reps per side in Reps.

Core (hernia-friendly)

Belly Breathing with Brace

The foundation. It retrains your deep core (transverse abdominis) and diaphragm with almost no pressure on the hernia.

Setup

Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat. Put one hand on your belly and one on your chest. A pillow under your head is fine.

Steps

  1. Breathe in slowly through your nose for about 4 seconds. Your belly rises under your hand and your chest stays mostly still.
  2. Breathe out through pursed lips for about 6 seconds and let your belly fall.
  3. At the end of the exhale, gently draw your belly button toward your spine. Hold for 3–5 seconds while breathing normally.
  4. Relax completely, then repeat.
Dose: 10 breaths, 1–2 sets. Log the breaths in Reps.

Stop if you feel dizzy. Slow the breathing down.

Pelvic Tilt

Teaches your lower abs to work with a tiny, controlled motion. No crunching.

Setup

Lie on your back with knees bent, feet flat and hip-width apart, arms by your sides. Notice the small arch under your low back.

Steps

  1. Breathe in to get ready.
  2. Breathe out and gently brace. Tip your pelvis so your low back presses flat into the floor and your tailbone curls up slightly.
  3. Hold 3–5 seconds while you keep breathing.
  4. Release back to neutral.
Dose: 10 reps, 2 sets.

Your butt stays on the floor. This isn't a bridge. Keep the motion small.

Heel Slide

Adds leg movement while your core holds steady. It's the first step toward dead bugs.

Setup

Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat. Find the gentle brace.

Steps

  1. Breathe out and slowly slide one heel along the floor until the leg is nearly straight.
  2. Breathe in and slide it back.
  3. Switch legs. Your pelvis and low back don't rock or arch.
Dose: 8–10 per leg, 2 sets. A towel or sock under the heel helps it slide.

If your back arches or your belly pushes up, shorten the slide.

Dead Bug

Great core control with a flat, supported back. Start with legs only and add the arms later.

Setup

Lie on your back. Lift your legs one at a time to a "tabletop" with knees over hips and shins parallel to the floor. Arms point at the ceiling, or rest on the floor at first.

Steps

  1. Breathe out and brace. Slowly lower one heel to tap the floor, keeping the knee bent.
  2. Breathe in and bring it back to tabletop. Switch legs.
  3. Later: straighten the leg further. After that, lower the opposite arm overhead at the same time.
Dose: 6–8 per side, 2 sets.

The moment your low back lifts off the floor, or your belly domes, shrink the range. Lift your legs into tabletop one at a time, never both together.

Bird Dog

Trains your back and deep core together. Gravity isn't pushing on your belly in this position.

Setup

Get on hands and knees with hands under shoulders and knees under hips. Keep your back flat like a tabletop.

Steps

  1. Breathe out and brace. Slide one leg straight back along the floor, then lift it to hip height.
  2. When that's easy, also reach the opposite arm forward.
  3. Hold 3–5 seconds, return, and switch sides.
Dose: 6–8 per side, 2 sets.

Don't let your belly sag or your back arch. Pretend there's a glass of water on your low back.

Glute Bridge

Strong glutes take load off your back and belly. Keep it gentle while the brace holds.

Setup

Lie on your back with knees bent, feet flat and hip-width apart, close enough that your fingertips brush your heels.

Steps

  1. Breathe out, brace, and do a small pelvic tilt.
  2. Squeeze your glutes and lift your hips until shoulders, hips and knees make a straight line.
  3. Hold 2–3 seconds while breathing. Lower slowly, one vertebra at a time.
Dose: 10 reps, 2 sets.

Don't push your hips past the straight line. Watch your scar line for doming as you rise, and if you see it, go lower.

Pallof Press

Anti-rotation: your core resists a sideways pull with no crunching. You can do it on the cable machine at the gym.

Setup

Set a cable handle (or a resistance band) at chest height. Stand side-on to the machine with feet hip-width apart and knees soft. Hold the handle with both hands at the center of your chest. Step out until there's tension.

Steps

  1. Brace gently. Breathe out and press the handle straight out in front of your chest.
  2. Hold 2–3 seconds. The cable tries to twist you toward the machine. Don't let it, and keep your hips and shoulders square.
  3. Breathe in and bring it back to your chest. Do all your reps, then turn around and do the other side.
Dose: 8–10 per side, 2 sets. Start light, the lightest plate. Log the weight and reps per side.

If you have to hold your breath to resist, it's too heavy.

Walking

Builds endurance, helps healing, and works your core in an upright, natural way.

How

  1. Walk tall: head up, ribs stacked over hips, arms swinging easily.
  2. Keep a pace where you can still talk in full sentences.
  3. Build time before speed or hills. Add 5 minutes a week.
Dose: 10–30 minutes. Log the minutes in Reps.

Chest

Machine Chest Press

Setup

Set the seat so the handles line up with the middle of your chest. Sit with your back flat on the pad and feet flat.

Steps

  1. Grip the handles and breathe out as you press them straight out.
  2. Stop just short of locking your elbows.
  3. Breathe in and bring them back slowly until you feel a light stretch across your chest.
Dose: 2–3 sets of 8–12. Pick a weight where the last 2 reps are hard but your form stays clean.

Form: Shoulders stay down and back against the pad. Don't let them roll forward.

Breathe out on every push or pull and never hold your breath. Stop if you see a bulge or doming at your scar.

Machine Fly

Setup

Set the seat so the handles are at chest height and your arms are about level with your shoulders. Back flat on the pad.

Steps

  1. Grab the handles with a slight bend in your elbows, and lock that bend in.
  2. Breathe out and sweep your arms together in front of you, like hugging a big tree.
  3. Squeeze for a second, then breathe in and open slowly until you feel a stretch. Don't go past where your shoulders are comfortable.
Dose: 2–3 sets of 10–12. Pick a weight where the last 2 reps are hard but your form stays clean.

Form: Light weight and slow, especially on the way back open. The stretch is where shoulders get hurt.

Breathe out on every push or pull and never hold your breath. Stop if you see a bulge or doming at your scar.

Machine Reverse Fly

Setup

Sit facing the machine with your chest against the pad. Set the handles so your arms are at shoulder height, out in front of you.

Steps

  1. Grip the handles with a soft bend in your elbows.
  2. Breathe out and open your arms out to the sides, squeezing your shoulder blades together.
  3. Pause, then breathe in and return slowly.
Dose: 2–3 sets of 12–15. Pick a weight where the last 2 reps are hard but your form stays clean.

Form: Go light. This works small rear-shoulder muscles. Don't shrug your shoulders up to your ears.

Breathe out on every push or pull and never hold your breath. Stop if you see a bulge or doming at your scar.

Arms

Seated Preacher Curls

Setup

Adjust the seat so your armpits sit snug on top of the pad and the backs of your upper arms rest flat on it.

Steps

  1. Grip the handle (or bar) with palms up.
  2. Breathe out and curl it up toward your shoulders. Your upper arms stay glued to the pad.
  3. Breathe in and lower slowly until your arms are almost straight.
Dose: 2–3 sets of 8–12. Pick a weight where the last 2 reps are hard but your form stays clean.

Form: Don't swing your body or lift your elbows off the pad. Control the lowering. That's where most of the work happens.

Breathe out on every push or pull and never hold your breath. Stop if you see a bulge or doming at your scar.

Machine Triceps Extension

Setup

Set the seat so your elbows line up with the machine's pivot point, the axle the arm swings around, with your upper arms resting on the pad.

Steps

  1. Grip the handles with your elbows bent.
  2. Breathe out and straighten your arms by pushing the handles forward and down.
  3. Squeeze the backs of your arms, then breathe in and let the handles come back slowly.
Dose: 2–3 sets of 10–12. Pick a weight where the last 2 reps are hard but your form stays clean.

Form: Only your forearms move. Don't let your elbows flare out or your shoulders hunch forward.

Breathe out on every push or pull and never hold your breath. Stop if you see a bulge or doming at your scar.

Machine Triceps Press

Setup

Sit tall with your back on the pad and the handles at your sides, about level with your lower ribs. Use the thigh pad if there is one.

Steps

  1. Grip the handles and keep your elbows pointing back, not out.
  2. Breathe out and press the handles down until your arms are straight beside your hips.
  3. Breathe in and let them rise slowly to about a 90° elbow bend. No lower.
Dose: 2–3 sets of 8–12. Pick a weight where the last 2 reps are hard but your form stays clean.

Form: Keep your shoulders down and your chest up. Don't let them roll forward at the top.

Breathe out on every push or pull and never hold your breath. Stop if you see a bulge or doming at your scar.

Shoulders/Lats

Machine Shoulder Press

Setup

Set the seat so the handles start about level with your shoulders. Back flat against the pad and feet planted.

Steps

  1. Grip the handles and breathe out as you press them overhead.
  2. Stop just short of locking your elbows.
  3. Breathe in and lower slowly back to shoulder height.
Dose: 2–3 sets of 8–12. Pick a weight where the last 2 reps are hard but your form stays clean.

Form: Don't arch your low back off the pad to push more weight. If you have to, it's too heavy. Overhead pressing loads the core, so exhale every rep.

Breathe out on every push or pull and never hold your breath. Stop if you see a bulge or doming at your scar.

Machine Lateral Arm Raise

Setup

Sit with the pads resting on the outside of your upper arms, near your elbows. Line your shoulders up with the machine's pivot points.

Steps

  1. Grip the handles lightly. Your elbows do the lifting.
  2. Breathe out and raise your arms out to the sides up to shoulder height. Not higher.
  3. Breathe in and lower slowly.
Dose: 2–3 sets of 12–15. Pick a weight where the last 2 reps are hard but your form stays clean.

Form: Go light and don't shrug. If your shoulders creep up toward your ears, drop the weight.

Breathe out on every push or pull and never hold your breath. Stop if you see a bulge or doming at your scar.

Back

Cable Lat Pull Down

Setup

Set the thigh pad so it holds your legs down snugly. Grab the bar a bit wider than your shoulders and sit down with your arms straight up.

Steps

  1. Lean back very slightly and lift your chest.
  2. Breathe out and pull the bar down to your upper chest, driving your elbows down and back.
  3. Breathe in and let the bar rise slowly until your arms are straight again.
Dose: 2–3 sets of 8–12. Pick a weight where the last 2 reps are hard but your form stays clean.

Form: Never pull behind your neck. Don't yank with your body. If you have to rock back to move it, drop the weight.

Breathe out on every push or pull and never hold your breath. Stop if you see a bulge or doming at your scar.

Cable Row

Setup

Sit on the bench with your feet on the platform and knees slightly bent. Grab the handle and sit up tall with your arms straight.

Steps

  1. Brace gently and keep your torso upright.
  2. Breathe out and pull the handle to your belly, elbows close to your sides, squeezing your shoulder blades together.
  3. Breathe in and let your arms go straight slowly. Don't let the weight pull you forward.
Dose: 2–3 sets of 8–12. Pick a weight where the last 2 reps are hard but your form stays clean.

Form: Don't rock back and forth to move the weight. Your torso stays still and your arms and back do the work.

Breathe out on every push or pull and never hold your breath. Stop if you see a bulge or doming at your scar.

Back Extension Machine

Setup

Set the seat and foot plate so the back pad sits across your shoulder blades and your hips are snug against the seat back.

Steps

  1. Start leaning slightly forward with your arms crossed on your chest.
  2. Breathe out and push back against the pad until you're upright. Don't go into a big backbend.
  3. Breathe in and return slowly.
Dose: 2–3 sets of 10–15. Pick a weight where the last 2 reps are hard but your form stays clean.

Form: Light and controlled. Stop at upright, because leaning way back strains your low back.

Breathe out on every push or pull and never hold your breath. Stop if you see a bulge or doming at your scar.
